1. Anatomy & Physiology

  • Anatomy: study of structure and structural relationships.
  • Physiology: study of function.
  • Gross anatomy: structures visible without a microscope.
  • Microscopic anatomy: cytology (cells) and histology (tissues).
  • Developmental anatomy: structural changes through life; embryology emphasizes prenatal development.
Key idea: structure and function are interdependent.

2. Levels of Organization

Chemical → Cellular → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism
  • Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living things.
  • Tissues are groups of specialized cells and surrounding material working together.
  • Organs contain two or more tissue types.
  • Organ systems contain related organs working toward a common function.

4. Life Processes

  • Metabolism: all chemical reactions; includes catabolism and anabolism.
  • Responsiveness: detecting and responding to change.
  • Movement: motion of body, organs, cells, or cell parts.
  • Growth: increase in size, cell number, or extracellular material.
  • Differentiation: specialization of cells.
  • Reproduction: formation of new cells or a new individual.

5. Homeostasis & Feedback

Homeostasis is dynamic maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions.

Stimulus → Receptor → Control Center → Effector → Response
  • Negative feedback: reverses a deviation. Examples: temperature, blood pressure, blood glucose.
  • Positive feedback: reinforces a change until an endpoint. Examples: childbirth and blood clotting.
  • Extracellular fluid: fluid outside cells; includes plasma and interstitial fluid.

7. Planes, Cavities & Regions

  • Sagittal: right/left; midsagittal makes equal halves.
  • Frontal: anterior/posterior.
  • Transverse: superior/inferior.
  • Dorsal cavity: cranial + vertebral.
  • Ventral cavity: thoracic + abdominopelvic.
  • Pleural: lungs; pericardial: heart; peritoneal: many abdominopelvic organs.
Nine regions: R hypochondriac • epigastric • L hypochondriac / R lumbar • umbilical • L lumbar / R inguinal • hypogastric • L inguinal.
