Muscles of the Thorax and Shoulder That Move the

  • The muscles of the head that move the tongue are important in mastication and speech.
  • Muscles of the thorax that move the humerus originate for the most part on the scapula (scapular muscles).
  • These muscles are also involved in deglutition.
  • The remaining muscles originate on the axial skeleton (axial muscles).

Muscles of the Arm That Move the Radius and Ulna

  • Muscles of the anterior neck that assist in deglutition and speech, called suprahyoid muscles, are located above the hyoid bone.
  • Muscles of the arm that move the radius and ulna are involved in flexion and extension at the elbow joint.
  • The anterior neck also contains infrahyoid muscles, which along with suprahyoid muscles, help stabilize the hyoid bone.
  • These muscles are organized into flexor and extensor compartments.

Muscles of the Forearm That Move the Wrist, Hand,

  • Muscles of the neck that move the head alter the position of the head.
  • Muscles of the forearm that move the wrist, hand, thumb, and digits are many and varied.
  • These muscles also help balance the head on the vertebral column.
  • Those muscles that act on the digits are called extrinsic muscles.

Muscles of the Palm That Move the Digits—Intrinsic

  • Muscles of the abdomen help contain and protect the abdominal viscera and move the vertebral column.
  • The muscles of the palm that move the digits (intrinsic muscles) are important in skilled activities and provide the ability to grasp and manipulate objects processly.
  • These muscles also compress the abdomen and help produce the force required for defecation, urination, vomiting, and childbirth.

Muscles of the Neck and Back That Move the

  • Muscles of the thorax used in breathing alter the size of the thoracic cavity so that inhalation and exhalation can occur.
  • Muscles of the neck that move the head alter its position and help balance it on the vertebral column.
  • These muscles also assist in venous return of blood to the heart.
  • Muscles of the neck that move the vertebral column are quite complex because they have multiple origins and insertions and because there is considerable overlap among them.

Muscles of the Pelvic Floor That Support Pelvic

  • Muscles of the pelvic floor support the pelvic viscera and resist the thrust that accompanies increases in intra-abdominal pressure.
  • These muscles also function as sphincters at the anorectal junction, urethra, and vagina.

Muscles of the Perineum

  • The perineum is the region of the truck inferior to the pelvic diaphragm.
  • Muscles of the perineum assist in urination, erection of the penis and clitoris, ejaculation, and defecation.

Muscles of the Thorax That Move the Pectoral Girdle

  • These muscles also move the scapula to increase the range of motion of the humerus.

Muscles of the Gluteal Region That Move the Femur

  • Muscles of the gluteal region that move the femur originate for the most part of the pelvic girdle and insert on the femur.
  • These muscles are larger and more powerful than comparable muscles in the upper limb.

Muscles of the Thigh That Move the Femur, Tibia,

  • Muscles of the thigh that move the femur, tibia, and fibula are separated into medial (adductor), anterior (extensor), and posterior (flexor) compartments.

Muscles of the Leg That Move the Foot and Toes

  • Muscles of the leg that move the foot and toes are divided into anterior, lateral, and posterior compartments.

Intrinsic Muscles of the Foot That Move the Toes

  • Intrinsic muscles of the foot that move the toes, unlike those of the hand, are limited to the functions of support and locomotion.
