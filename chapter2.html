1. Matter, Energy & Atomic Structure

  • Matter: anything with mass that occupies space.
  • Energy: capacity to do work; potential energy is stored, kinetic energy is energy of motion.
  • Atom: smallest unit of an element retaining its properties.
  • Proton: + charge; neutron: no charge; electron: − charge.
  • Atomic number: number of protons. Mass number: protons + neutrons.
  • Isotopes: same element, different neutron number.

2. Chemical Bonds & Reactions

  • Ionic bond: attraction between oppositely charged ions after electron transfer.
  • Covalent bond: atoms share electrons; may be nonpolar or polar.
  • Hydrogen bond: weak attraction involving partially charged hydrogen; important in water and biological molecules.
  • Synthesis: smaller reactants form a larger product. Decomposition: larger substance breaks down. Exchange: parts are swapped.
  • Reversible reactions: can proceed in either direction depending on conditions.

3. Water, Solutions, Acids & Bases

  • Water is an excellent solvent, participates in reactions, has high heat capacity, and lubricates tissues.
  • Solvent: dissolving medium. Solute: dissolved substance.
  • Acid: proton donor; base: proton acceptor.
  • pH: measures hydrogen-ion concentration; 7 neutral, below 7 acidic, above 7 basic.
  • Buffers: resist abrupt pH changes by binding or releasing H⁺.

4. Carbohydrates

  • Contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen and are a major energy source.
  • Monosaccharides: one sugar unit; glucose is a major example.
  • Disaccharides: two monosaccharides joined by dehydration synthesis.
  • Polysaccharides: long chains; glycogen is the main carbohydrate storage form in humans.
  • Hydrolysis: adds water to split larger molecules.

5. Lipids

  • Lipids contain relatively little oxygen and are largely hydrophobic.
  • Triglyceride: glycerol + three fatty acids; major energy-storage lipid.
  • Phospholipids: major structural components of cell membranes.
  • Steroids: four-ring lipids; cholesterol is a precursor for steroid hormones and contributes to membranes.
  • Saturated fatty acids: no carbon-carbon double bonds; unsaturated: one or more double bonds.

6. Proteins & Enzymes

  • Proteins are polymers built from amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
  • Protein shape determines function; heat, pH, or chemicals can cause denaturation.
  • Enzymes: protein catalysts that lower activation energy and are not consumed by the reaction.
  • Substrate: reactant that binds an enzyme's active site.
  • Protein roles include structural, regulatory, contractile, immunological, transport, and catalytic functions.

7. Nucleic Acids & ATP

  • Nucleotide: nitrogenous base + five-carbon sugar + phosphate.
  • DNA: stores hereditary information; typically double stranded and contains deoxyribose and thymine.
  • RNA: participates in protein synthesis; typically single stranded and contains ribose and uracil.
  • Purines: adenine and guanine. Pyrimidines: cytosine, thymine, uracil.
  • ATP: adenine + ribose + three phosphate groups; hydrolysis of the terminal phosphate releases usable cellular energy.
