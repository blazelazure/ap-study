Lecture Focus

  • axial skeleton divisions and bone markings
  • skull, vertebral column, and thorax

Core Concepts

  • Bones protect soft body parts and make movement possible; they also serve as landmarks for locating parts of other body systems.
  • The musculoskeletal system is composed of the bones, joints, and muscles working together.

Divisions of the Skeletal System (see Table 7.1)

  • The axial skeleton consists of bones arranged along the longitudinal axis.
  • The parts of the axial skeleton are the skull, auditory ossicles, hyoid bone, vertebral column, sternum, and ribs.
  • The appendicular skeleton consists of the bones of the girdles and the upper and lower limbs.
  • The parts of the appendicular skeleton are the pectoral girdles, bones of the upper limbs, pelvic girdles, and bones of the lower limbs.

Types of Bones

  • On the basis of shape, bones are classified as long, short, flat, irregular, or sesamoid.
  • Sesamoid bones develop in tendons or ligaments.
  • Sutural bones are found within the sutures of some cranial bones.

Bone Surface Markings

  • Surface markings are structural features visible on the surfaces of bones.

Skull: An Overview

  • The 22 bones of the skull include cranial cavity bones and facial bones.
  • The eight cranial cavity bones are the frontal, parietal (2), temporal (2), occipital, sphenoid, and ethmoid.
  • The 14 facial bones are the nasal (2), maxillae (2), zygomatic (2), lacrimal (2), palatine (2), inferior nasal concha bones (2), vomer, and mandible.

Cranial Cavity Bones

  • The frontal bone forms the forehead (the anterior part of the cranial cavity.
  • The frontal bone also forms the roofs of the orbits and most of the anterior part of the cranial base.
  • The parietal bones form the greater portion of the sides of the c ranial cavity.
  • The parietal bones also form most of the roof of the cranial cavity.
  • The temporal bones form the inferior lateral aspects of the cranial cavity.
  • The temporal bones also form part of the cranial base.

Vertebral Column

  • The ethmoid bone is located in the anterior part of the cranial base medial to the orbits.
  • The ethmoid bone is anterior to the sphenoid and posterior to the nasal bones.

Facial Bones

  • The nasal bones from the bridge of the nose.
  • The lacrimal bones are posterior and lateral to the nasal bones and form a part of the medial wall of each orbit.
  • The palatine bones form the posterior portion of hard palate, part of the floor and lateral wall of the nasal cavity, and a small portion of the floors of the orbits.
  • The inferior nasal concha bones form a part of the inferior lateral wall of the nasal cavity and project into the nasal cavity.
  • The vomer forms the inferior portion of the nasal septum.
  • The maxillae form the upper jawbone.

Special Features of the Skull

  • The nasal septum consists of the vomer, perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, and septal nasal cartilage.
  • The nasal septum divides the nasal cavity into left and right sides.
  • Seven skull bones form each of the orbits.
  • The foramina of the skull bones provide passages for nerves and blood vessels.
  • Sutures are immovable joints that connect most bones of the skull.
  • Examples are the coronal, sagittal, lambdoid, and squamous sutures.

Hyoid Bone

  • The hyoid bone is a U-shaped bone that does not articulate with any other bone.
  • The vertebral column, sternum, and ribs constitute the skeleton of the body’s trunk.
  • The 26 bones of the adult vertebral column are the cervical vertebrae (7), the thoracic vertebrae (12), the lumbar vertebrae (5), the sacrum (5 fused sacral vertebrae), and the coccyx (usually 4 fused coccygeal vertebrae).
  • The adult vertebral column contains four normal curves (cervical, thoracic, lumbar, and sacral) that provide strength, support, and balance.
  • Each vertebra usually consists of a body, vertebral arch, and seven processes.
  • Vertebrae in the different regions of the column vary in size, shape, and detail.

Vertebral Regions

  • The cervical vertebrae (C1–C7) are smaller than all other vertebrae except those that form the coccyx.
  • The first two cervical vertebrae are the atlas (C1) and the axis (C2).
  • The thoracic vertebrae (T1–T12) are considerably larger and s tronger than cervical vertebrae.
  • The thoracic vertebrae articulate with the ribs.
  • The lumbar vertebrae (L1–L5) are the largest and strongest of the unfused bones in the vertebral column.
  • The various projections of the lumbar vertebrae are short and thick.

Thorax

  • The thoracic skeleton consists of the sternum, ribs, costal cartilages, and thoracic vertebrae.
  • The thoracic cage protects vital organs in the chest area and upper abdomen.
  • The sternum is located in the center of the anterior thoracic wall.
  • The sternum consists of the manubrium, body, and xiphoid process.
  • The twelve pairs of ribs give structural support to the sides of the thoracic cavity.
  • The three types of ribs are the true ribs, vertebrochondral ribs, and floating ribs.
