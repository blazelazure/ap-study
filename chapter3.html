1. Generalized Cell & Plasma Membrane

  • A generalized cell has three major regions: plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus.
  • The plasma membrane is a selective barrier and participates in communication.
  • The lipid bilayer contains phospholipids, cholesterol, and glycolipids.
  • Integral/transmembrane proteins: embedded in or span the bilayer; peripheral proteins: attached to a membrane surface.
  • Glycocalyx: carbohydrate-rich cell surface formed from glycoproteins and glycolipids.

2. Membrane Transport

  • Passive processes: no direct ATP input; include simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, and osmosis.
  • Diffusion rate is affected by concentration gradient, temperature, mass, surface area, and diffusion distance.
  • Osmosis: net water movement through a selectively permeable membrane toward lower water / higher solute concentration.
  • Isotonic: RBC unchanged; hypotonic: swelling/hemolysis; hypertonic: shrinking/crenation.
  • Active transport: uses energy to move substances against a gradient; Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is a major example.

3. Vesicular Transport

  • Endocytosis: material enters in vesicles.
  • Receptor-mediated endocytosis: selective uptake through membrane receptors.
  • Phagocytosis: uptake of solid particles; “cell eating.”
  • Pinocytosis/bulk-phase endocytosis: uptake of extracellular fluid.
  • Exocytosis: vesicles fuse with membrane and release contents. Transcytosis: endocytosis on one side and exocytosis on the other.

4. Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton & Organelles

Ribosome
Protein synthesis.
Rough ER
Secretory, membrane, and organellar proteins.
Smooth ER
Lipids/steroids, detoxification, Ca²⁺ storage.
Golgi
Modifies, sorts, packages.
Lysosome
Intracellular digestion and recycling.
Peroxisome
Oxidation and H₂O₂ detoxification.
Proteasome
Degrades damaged/unneeded proteins.
Mitochondrion
Aerobic respiration and most ATP production.
Centrosome
Microtubule-organizing center.
Cilia / Flagella
Move fluid / move the cell.

Cytoskeleton: microfilaments, intermediate filaments, and microtubules; provides shape, organization, and movement.

5. Nucleus & Genetic Material

  • The nucleus contains DNA organized as chromosomes; genes are hereditary units within DNA.
  • Nuclear envelope: double membrane with pores regulating exchange with cytoplasm.
  • Chromatin: diffuse DNA-protein complex during interphase.
  • Nucleolus: produces ribosomal subunits.

6. Protein Synthesis & Gene Expression

DNA —transcription→ RNA —translation→ Protein
  • Transcription: occurs in the nucleus; RNA polymerase copies DNA information into RNA.
  • mRNA: carries the coding message; rRNA: forms part of ribosomes; tRNA: brings amino acids and uses an anticodon.
  • Translation: occurs at ribosomes in cytoplasm; mRNA codons determine amino-acid sequence.
  • AUG: start codon and methionine; stop codons terminate translation.
  • Introns are removed and exons are spliced together before mature mRNA exits the nucleus.

7. Cell Division, Death & Aging

  • Somatic cell division: mitosis + cytokinesis → two genetically identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number.
  • Meiosis: reproductive division that reduces chromosome number by half to form gametes.
  • Mitosis: prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase; cytokinesis divides cytoplasm.
  • Apoptosis: orderly programmed cell death; necrosis: uncontrolled cell death from injury/disease.
  • Cell aging involves cumulative damage, altered gene expression/protein function, and limits on cell division such as telomere shortening.
