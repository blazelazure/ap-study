Lecture Focus

  • pectoral and pelvic girdles
  • bones of the upper and lower limbs

Pectoral (Shoulder) Girdle

  • Each of the body’s two pectoral girdles consists of a clavicle and scapula.
  • Each pectoral girdle attaches an upper limb to the axial skeleton.
  • The clavicle lies horizontally across the anterior part of the thorax superior to the first rib.
  • The medial end of the clavicle articulates with the manubrium of the sternum; the lateral end of the clavicle articulates with the acromion of the scapula.
  • The scapula is situated in the superior part of the posterior thorax between the levels of the second and seventh ribs.
  • The scapula articulates with the clavicle and the head of the humerus.

Upper Limb

  • Each of the two upper limbs contains 30 bones.
  • The bones of each upper limb include the humerus, the ulna, the radius, the carpals, the metacarpals, and the phalanges.
  • The humerus is the longest and largest bone of the upper limb.
  • The humerus articulates proximally with the scapula and distally with the ulna and radius.
  • The ulna is located on the medial aspect of the forearm and is longer than the radius.
  • The radius is the smaller bone of the forearm and is located on the lateral aspect of the forearm.

Pelvic Girdle

  • The pelvic girdle consists of two hip bones.
  • Each hip bone consists of three parts: the ilium, pubis, and ischium.

  • The hip bones, sacrum, and pubic symphysis form the bony pelvis.
  • It supports the vertebral column and pelvic viscera and attaches the lower limbs to the axial skeleton.
  • The ilium is the superior portion of the hip bone.

Greater and Lesser Pelves

  • The greater pelvis is separated from the lesser pelvis by the pelvic inlet.
  • The lesser pelvis surrounds the pelvic cavity and houses the rectum and urinary bladder in both genders, the vagina and cervix of the uterus in females, and the prostate and seminal glands in males.
  • The greater pelvis is the lower portion of the abdomen that is situated superior to the pelvic inlet.
  • It contains the superior portion of the urinary bladder (when full) and the lower intestines in both genders and the uterus, uterine tubes, and ovaries in the female.

Comparison of Female and Male Pelves

  • Bones of the male skeleton are generally larger and heavier than bones of the female skeleton.
  • They also have more prominent markings for muscle attachments.
  • The female pelvis is adapted for pregnancy and childbirth.

Lower Limb

  • Each of the two lower limbs contains 30 bones.
  • The bones of each lower limb include the femur, the patella, the tibia, the fibula, the tarsals, the metatarsals, and the phalanges.
  • The femur is the longest, heaviest, and strongest bone in the body.
  • The patella is a small, triangular bone located anterior to the knee joint.
  • The tibia is the larger, medial, weight-bearing bone of the leg.
  • The fibula is parallel and lateral to the tibia, but is considerably smaller.

Development of the Skeletal System

  • Most bones form from mesoderm by intramembranous or endochondral ossification; much of the skeleton of the skull arises from ectoderm.
